How to use the editor

Quick start

  1. A world is generated when the editor opens. Wait for the pipeline to finish: the bar at the top of the globe shows each stage as it runs. The climate takes the longest, about a minute.
  2. Turn the globe by dragging and zoom with the scroll wheel, or switch to Map at the top to see the whole world flat. G switches between the two.
  3. Pick a layer in the Layers tab to show it: elevation, temperature, rain, biome and so on. Click a cell with Move to read every layer at that cell on the right.
  4. Change the world in the World and Climate tabs, or sculpt and paint it with the tools on the globe. Everything that depends on your change reruns by itself.
  5. Export saves the world to a file.

The screen

  • Left panel, four tabs. Layers: what is shown and edited. World: seed, sea level, size, plates, continents, erosion and plumes. Climate: day length, spin direction, axial tilt, sunlight, air pressure and greenhouse strength (Earth's are 1 bar and ×1), an overall warmer or colder offset, whether the ice sheets shape the climate, and Quick preview. Untick the first, or tick Quick preview, for much faster builds while you experiment; reverse them for the finished world. Pipeline: the stages that build the world, with each one's state.
  • Tools float at the top left of the globe. They change with the layer: sculpting tools for heights, paint and clear for categories, set motion and plumes for plates.
  • Top bar. Globe or Map. Show holds the overlays: cell edges, relief shading, contour lines, latitude and longitude, kept cells, plate motion arrows and storm tracks.
  • Time (top right of the globe) sets the month and the hour. Tick Day and night to see the real sunlight, with polar night and midnight sun; Play runs time forward. The legend under it explains the colours of the layer shown.
  • Right panel. Everything about the cell you clicked, where each value came from, and a Keep box per layer.

Getting around

On the globe, drag to turn it and scroll or pinch to zoom. On the map, drag to pan (it wraps round east and west) and scroll to zoom. While a brush tool is active, the right mouse button turns or pans instead, so the left one can paint.

How the world is built

  • The pipeline builds every layer from the ones before it, in order: plates → boundaries → tectonic height → sea level → erosion → rock → sunlight and temperature → pressure and winds → ocean currents → storms → humidity and rain → glaciers and ice sheets → lakes and rivers → biomes. When you change something, only the stages after it rerun.
  • Each stage can be Auto (simulated), Hand (left exactly as you made it) or Off.
  • Your edits sit on top of what a stage makes, so they survive reruns. Keep a cell to stop the stages changing it at all.
  • The climate is physical, not painted: sunlight, two layers of air, winds, a three-layer ocean with currents and sea ice, evaporation, rain and rivers. So a mountain range casts a rain shadow, a warm current warms its coast and a tilted planet gets harsher seasons, by themselves.

Presets

The Preset menu at the top of the World tab sets the World and Climate settings for a kind of world (a young Earth, a snowball, an ocean world, a fast spinner and more) and rebuilds it, keeping your seed. Earth-like puts back the defaults.

Start from a generated world

Click New generated world in the World tab (twice, to confirm). Change the seed, number of plates, continental share or plumes and the world rebuilds. Then sculpt and paint on top.

Start from a blank world

  1. Click New blank world twice. You get one still plate of ocean floor.
  2. Add plates. Pick the Plate layer, choose Paint P, pick Plate 2 in the bar under the globe and click once where that plate should start. Do the same with Plate 3, Plate 4 and so on. One dab per plate is enough: the plates grow from your dabs until they meet. Paint more of a plate to steer its shape. Plate 1 is the ocean you started with; dab it somewhere too if you want to keep it.
  3. Set their motion. Still on the Plate layer, choose Set motion M and drag from inside a plate in the direction it should travel. A longer drag is faster; a click stops it. Where plates meet, the boundaries work out collisions, trenches, ridges and rifts.
  4. Draw continents. Pick the Crust layer, choose Paint, pick Continental and paint the land masses. (Or tick Generate continents in World to have them made for you.)
  5. Sculpt. Pick Elevation and use Raise, Lower, Smooth and Flatten for anything the tectonics didn't give you.

The layers

  • Tectonics: Plate, Crust, Boundary, Convergence, Crust age, Tectonic height. Paint Boundary cells to force a feature, for example Collision for a mountain belt. Add plume H on the Plate layer places a mantle plume: a dome, a volcano and an island chain trailing along the plate's motion. Physical plate motion (World) works out how plates move from the forces on them; Set motion overrides it.
  • Land: Elevation, Sediment, Drainage, Landform, Feature. Erosion carves valleys and drops sediment in basins and deltas; your own sculpting erodes too, so keep cells you want left exactly as made. Volcanoes, calderas, hot springs, passes and caves appear on Feature by themselves.
  • Rock: Rock and Resource (ores, gold, gems, coal, oil, gas). Paint Rock to change the geology; resources and karst follow.
  • Temperature: Sunlight, Temperature for the year, January, July and now, Seasonal range, Köppen climate.
  • Air: Pressure and Wind (January and July) with arrows, Upper wind, Rising air. Day length and Spins backward change the circulation: slow spinners get one wide cell per hemisphere, fast ones up to five.
  • Ocean: Sea temperature, Ocean current, Upwelling and sinking, Sea ice (seasonal or permanent), Sea ice (now) for the month on the Time slider, Current warmth, Low cloud, Warm layer depth. The warmth currents bring to the coasts is already in the temperature layers.
  • Weather and water: Tropical cyclones, Cyclone rain, Winter storm tracks (tick Storm tracks under Show), Humidity, Rain for the year, January and July, Snow cover (now) for the month on the Time slider (snow builds up below freezing and melts with warmth), Lakes, Rivers, Runoff, and Biome.
  • Ice: Ice thickness. Ice sheets and glaciers build up wherever more snow falls in a year than the summers melt, spread into sheets kilometres thick at their centres, and keep themselves cold by their own height. They don't change through the year; the Sea ice (now) and Snow cover (now) layers do.
  • Lock biomes (Layers tab) keeps the biome map as it is, so later climate changes don't redraw it. It sets the Biomes stage to Hand; painting still works.
  • Your own layers: Add layer makes a number or category layer of your own (ley lines, kingdoms, anything) to paint by hand.

Saving

Your world is saved in this browser as you work. Export writes it to a .json file; Import loads one back. Use Export for anything you want to keep.
